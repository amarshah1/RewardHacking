- reasons why it would be worth continuing

- reasons why it would be not worth continuing
  - its hard to generate correct specifications/implementations in Verus—it seems like this will always be much worse than test case generation/implementations in Rust
  - the kind of reward hacks seem (maybe) contrived
    - If you give it a lot of few shot examples, you can convince it to do an if-else statement on the inputs
    - If you give it a lot of few shot examples, you can convince it to generate an underapproximation (i.e. end loop early)
    - in the wild, we see things that would meet our goal of reward hacking, but it doesnt feel as blatant: overflow issues, handling rounding in a different way, etc.
  - our datasets are kind of small -> hard to draw extrapolations from it
    - hard to expand dataset right now, because its hard to test against ground truth
